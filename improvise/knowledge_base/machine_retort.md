# Retort (in-container sterilisation)

## What it does / working principle
A retort is a pressure vessel that heats sealed containers (cans, pouches, trays, jars, bottles) to sterilising temperatures, holds them there, and then cools them under control. Retort pouch processing is commonly described at about 116–121 °C. The target is commercial sterility for low-acid foods, with *Clostridium botulinum* as the reference organism.

## Main types and when to choose each
| Type | Notes |
|---|---|
| Saturated steam (batch) | Air must be vented out first. Simple and efficient. Mainly suits rigid cans. |
| Water immersion (batch) | Containers are submerged, and air or steam provides overpressure. Gentle on pouches, trays and glass. |
| Water spray / cascade (batch) | Process water is sprayed or cascaded and heated by steam, with air overpressure. Widely used for pouches and trays. |
| Steam-air (batch) | Fan-mixed steam and air with overpressure. Suits flexible and semi-rigid packs. |
| Agitating (rotary end-over-end or reciprocating) | Faster heat penetration for convection-heating products. Speed of agitation becomes a critical factor. |
| Continuous rotary | High volume of similar round cans. The line stops if it fails. |
| Continuous hydrostatic | Very high volume, few formats. Large towers. Changes of format are costly. |

Batch retorts offer the most flexibility for many formats and SKUs. Continuous systems pay off on long runs of the same container.

## Key specifications to ask for
- Process type, design pressure and temperature, vessel diameter and length, baskets per batch
- Overpressure control, circulation (pump or fan) and its monitoring
- Heat distribution data, recorder and controller, and documentation for regulatory filing
- Loading system (manual or automatic basket loading), cycle time, utilities (steam, water, air)

## Sizing / selection notes
- **F0 concept:** a process's lethality is expressed as equivalent minutes at 121.1 °C, using a z-value (commonly 10 °C for *C. botulinum*). It is measured at the slowest-heating "cold spot".
- The required F0 and the scheduled process (time, temperature, critical factors) **must come from a qualified thermal process authority**, based on heat distribution and heat penetration studies of your product, pack and retort. Local regulations (for example US 21 CFR 113/114) also apply. Never take a value from a general note.
- Number of retorts ≈ (packs per hour × total cycle time) / packs per batch, plus loading time and spare capacity.

## Integration with the line
Upstream: filling and sealing, then loading into baskets or trays. Accumulation is needed because the retort works in batches. Downstream: unloading, drying, inspection, labelling. Keep processed and unprocessed baskets strictly separate, for example with heat-sensitive indicators and a physical layout that stops them mixing.

## Hygiene and safety notes
It is a pressure vessel, so follow inspection and safety-valve regimes. Door interlocks are needed. Cooling water quality (chlorination) matters because containers can draw in water while cooling.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Deformed or burst pouches | Overpressure profile wrong, especially during cooling | Pressure recipe, air supply |
| Temperature deviation | Steam supply, circulation failure, venting | Recorder chart, pump or fan alarm, steam pressure |
| Process deviation | Any critical factor out of limits | Hold product and refer to the process authority |
| Spoilage after processing | Under-processing, seal leaks, contaminated cooling water | Records, seal tests, water chlorine |
