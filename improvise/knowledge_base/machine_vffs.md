# Vertical Form Fill Seal (VFFS)

## What it does / working principle
A VFFS bagger makes bags from a flat roll of film, fills them and seals them. The film is pulled over a forming collar and wrapped around a vertical tube. A vertical (longitudinal) sealer joins the film edges, usually as a fin seal, sometimes as a lap seal. Horizontal jaws then seal the bottom of the tube. Product falls through the tube from a doser above. The jaws then seal the top of that bag, which also forms the bottom of the next bag, and a knife cuts the bags apart. The cut is set by a fixed bag length, or by a photo-eye that reads a print registration mark.

## Main types and when to choose each
- **Intermittent motion:** the film stops while the jaws close. It is simpler and suits most granules, powders and moderate speeds.
- **Continuous motion:** the jaws travel down with the film, so the film never stops. It is faster and suits snacks and other free-flowing product at high rates.
- **Bag styles:** pillow (most common), gusseted, quad-seal or flat-bottom (block bottom), stick packs on multi-lane machines.
- **Options:** gas flush (nitrogen) or air inflation to cushion fragile items, hole punch, tear notch, zipper.

## Key specifications to ask for
- Bag length and width range, and the list of former sizes
- Maximum reel width and diameter, film structure and its seal window
- Speed in bags/min at *your* bag size and product. Typical published figures run from tens of bags/min (intermittent) to a few hundred (continuous, small bags).
- Seal type: heat (constant heat or impulse) or ultrasonic. Jaw dwell time and pressure.
- Doser interface (multihead, auger, cup, liquid pump)

## Sizing / selection notes
- Bag width ≈ (film width − fin-seal allowance) / 2 for a pillow bag. Confirm against the former sizes on offer.
- The dosing system is usually the real speed limit. Check the doser's cycle time together with the bag length.
- Free-falling product sets a minimum cycle time, because the product needs time to drop through the tube.

## Integration with the line
The doser above signals "dump" and the bagger acknowledges "ready". Downstream equipment normally includes a checkweigher, a metal detector (sometimes in the throat, before the bag is formed), a date coder (TTO on the film), then case packing. The machine needs film splice alarms and low-film warnings.

## Hygiene and safety notes
Guard the jaw area with interlocked doors, because jaws and knives are crush and cut hazards. Clean product dust from the jaws, which causes seal contamination. Use washdown-rated designs for wet or fresh products.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Leaking seals | Product in the seal, temperature too low or too high, worn jaw serrations | Seal temperature and dwell, product fall timing, clean the jaws |
| Bag length drifting / cut through print | Registration eye dirty or mis-set, film slip on the pull belts | Clean and teach the photo-eye, check pull-belt pressure and vacuum |
| Film tracking off | Unbalanced reel, bad splice, worn dancer | Re-centre the reel, check the dancer and the brake |
| Product in the top seal | Dump too late, product too long in the tube | Adjust doser timing, add a delay, reduce bag fill |
