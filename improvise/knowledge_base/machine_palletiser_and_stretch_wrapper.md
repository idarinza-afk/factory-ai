# Palletiser and Stretch Wrapper

## What it does / working principle
A **palletiser** builds cases, bags or trays into layers in a set pattern, interlocked for stability, and stacks the layers on a pallet. Slip sheets go between layers if needed. A **stretch wrapper** then wraps stretch film around the load. The film is pre-stretched between powered rollers and applied under tension. Its elastic recovery holds the load together for transport.

## Main types and when to choose each
**Palletisers**
- **Conventional layer palletiser (low-level or high-level infeed):** fastest for consistent, rigid, single-SKU loads. One supplier quotes up to about 80 cases/min or 40 bags/min (typical upper figure).
- **Robotic (articulated arm or gantry):** flexible across SKUs, mixed pallets and odd packs. Lower peak speed. Often easier to integrate with the rest of the line.
- **Cobot:** light loads, low rates, small spaces.

**Stretch wrappers**
- **Turntable:** the load rotates. Simple and common.
- **Rotary arm:** the load stays still, which suits light or unstable loads.
- **Rotary ring:** high speed, balanced. One source mentions up to about 160 pallets/h.
- **Orbital:** the film wraps through the load, for long products.

## Key specifications to ask for
- Case and pallet sizes, patterns, load height and weight, number of SKUs
- Rate (cases/min, layers/min, pallets/h)
- Pallet and slip-sheet dispensers, pallet conveyor and accumulation
- Wrapper: pre-stretch ratio, film width, top-sheet option, containment force control, label applier

## Sizing / selection notes
- Pallets/h = cases/min × 60 / cases per pallet. Check that the wrapper and pallet conveyors keep up, including the time to change pallets.
- Stretch films are typically used at about 100–300% stretch, well below break.
- **Containment force** = wrap force × number of film layers. It should be even from top to bottom. One packaging supplier gives guide values by load weight (for example, a few pounds of force for light loads, more for heavy or unstable ones). Validate on your own loads.
- Wrap the film down onto the pallet base to tie the load to the pallet.

## Integration with the line
The palletiser sits after the case checkweigher and case labeller. Downstream: wrapper, pallet labeller (print-and-apply, often SSCC), then the warehouse, an AGV or a forklift pick-up station. Make sure there is enough full-pallet accumulation to cover forklift response times.

## Hygiene and safety notes
Robot cells and layer heads must be fenced, with interlocked access and light curtains at pallet exits (muting). Guard the turntable and ring wrappers. Plan safe forklift and pedestrian routes.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Unstable pallets | Weak pattern interlock, low containment force, bad cases | Pattern, film settings, case compression strength |
| Film breaks | Pre-stretch too high, sharp corners, film quality | Pre-stretch ratio, corner boards, film spec |
| Layer forming faults | Case gaps or bulging cases | Infeed spacing, case squareness |
| Robot mispicks | Gripper vacuum or cases not square | Gripper cups, vacuum, infeed alignment |
