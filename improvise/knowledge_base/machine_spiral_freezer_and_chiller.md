# Spiral Freezer and Chiller

## What it does / working principle
A spiral freezer or chiller carries product on a belt in a helix around a drum inside an insulated enclosure. Fans blow refrigerated air across the belt, and an evaporator coil removes the heat. Fast-moving air strips heat from the product surface far faster than still air. Fast freezing forms small ice crystals, which helps texture and limits drip loss. A long belt path in a small footprint gives long dwell times without a long tunnel.

## Main types and when to choose each
- **Drum-driven, low-tension belt:** the drum drives the belt by friction with little belt tension. Good for hygiene-critical and delicate products.
- **Self-stacking belt:** each tier rests on the one below, making an enclosed stack. High capacity in a small footprint.
- **Single or double drum:** a double drum gives longer dwell or higher capacity.
- **Chiller vs freezer:** a chiller works at higher air temperatures for cooked products going to chilled storage. A freezer brings the product core to frozen storage temperature.
- **Alternatives:** linear tunnel, impingement or cryogenic tunnel (LN2/CO2) for thin products or lower capital cost.

## Key specifications to ask for
One supplier's figures (typical): air at about −35 to −40 °C, a target core of −18 °C or colder, dwell from under 20 min for thin items to about an hour for thick patties, capacities from a few hundred to about 10,000 kg/h, belt widths of about 350–1,370 mm.
- Inlet and outlet product temperatures, product size and its weight loss (dehydration)
- Refrigerant (ammonia, CO2, HFC), refrigeration capacity (kW) at evaporating temperature
- Defrost method (sequential or staggered defrost helps longer runs), CIP or belt-wash system

## Sizing / selection notes
- Dwell time comes from the product's thickness and the required core temperature. Confirm it with freezing trials.
- Refrigeration load ≈ product mass flow × (sensible heat above freezing + latent heat + sensible heat below freezing), plus fan, infiltration and belt loads. Get the calculation from the supplier.
- Cooked-product chilling time and temperature targets come from your HACCP plan and local food-safety guidance.

## Integration with the line
- Upstream: cooking or forming, with an even feed to the belt.
- Downstream: packing, metal detection, cold storage.
- The spiral often marks a hygiene boundary between cooked and packing areas. Interlock the belt with upstream stoppages so product does not pile up.

## Hygiene and safety notes
Clean the belt, drum and evaporator regularly. CIP spray systems and belt washers are common. Ammonia systems need leak detection and safety procedures. Provide confined-space and lock-out rules for entry, and mark the person-trapped alarm and the doors that open from inside.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Product core too warm at exit | Frosted coil, overload, high inlet temperature | Defrost, loading, inlet product temperature |
| High weight loss | Airflow too high or long dwell | Fan speed, air temperature, dwell |
| Belt jams / flips | Tension, worn belt links, ice on drum | Belt tension, drum bars, defrost |
| Frequent defrost needed | Moist air ingress, product moisture | Door seals, infeed curtains |
