# CIP (Clean-in-Place) Systems

## What it does / working principle
CIP cleans pipelines, tanks, heat exchangers and process equipment without taking them apart. Cleaning solutions are circulated in a set sequence. Results depend on four interacting factors: temperature, chemical concentration, contact time and mechanical action (turbulent flow or spray impact). Pipe circuits are commonly designed for a minimum velocity of about 1.5 m/s to get turbulent flow.

## Main types and when to choose each
- **Single-use (single-pass) CIP:** solution goes to drain after use. Suits heavy soil or high-hygiene areas, and small systems.
- **Recovery (reuse) CIP:** detergent and final rinse water are collected in tanks and reused for the next cycle after concentration correction. Lower chemical and water use for larger plants.
- **Centralised vs decentralised (satellite) skids:** central skids save cost. Satellite skids shorten line lengths and reduce cross-contact risk.
- **Spray devices:** static spray balls for light soil, dynamic rotating jets for heavy soil or large vessels.

## Key specifications to ask for
- Number of circuits, tanks (water, caustic, acid, recovery), tank volumes
- Supply pump flow and pressure per circuit, return pump or eductor
- Heating (plate heat exchanger), dosing and conductivity control
- Instrumentation: flow, temperature, conductivity, and recording of critical parameters
- Automation, recipe management, validation documents

## Sizing / selection notes
- Flow rate = pipe cross-section area × target velocity (for example, ≥1.5 m/s). Size for the largest pipe in each circuit.
- Typical dairy guidance: caustic at about 0.5–1% at about 70–85 °C, nitric acid at about 0.5% at about 65 °C, plus a hot-water or chemical sanitising step (typical). Final values come from your chemical supplier and validation.
- Typical sequence: pre-rinse → caustic → intermediate rinse → acid → rinse → sanitise → final rinse (if required) → drain or air blow.

## Integration with the line
Routing is done with valve clusters, swing panels or mix-proof valves. These must physically separate CIP and product to avoid chemical contamination. Interlock CIP with production and log every cycle.

## Hygiene and safety notes
Verify cleaning with final-rinse conductivity, ATP swabs, visual inspection and riboflavin coverage tests for spray devices. Hot caustic and acid need PPE, splash guards and safe dosing. Vessels must have vacuum relief to prevent collapse when hot solution cools.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Soil remaining | Low flow or temperature, short contact time, blocked spray ball | Flowmeter, temperature trend, spray ball rotation and holes |
| Chemical carry-over | Short rinse, poor drainage, dead legs | Final-rinse conductivity, pipe slopes |
| Concentration out of range | Dosing pump or conductivity probe fault | Probe calibration, dosing pump |
| Long cycle times | Undersized pumps or heater | Pump curve, heater duty |
