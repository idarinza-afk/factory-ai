# Horizontal Form Fill Seal / Flow Wrapper

## What it does / working principle
A flow wrapper (horizontal form fill seal, HFFS) wraps a continuous tube of film around products that travel horizontally. An infeed (a lug chain or servo-driven belts) places each product at a fixed pitch. A forming box folds the film around the product. Fin wheels then make a longitudinal fin seal, and an end-seal head seals and cuts each pack. Flow wrappers make a fin seal and generally cannot make an overlap seal. A dancer keeps film tension steady, and a photo-eye reads register marks on printed film.

## Main types and when to choose each
- **Rotary end-seal jaws:** fastest, but the range of product sizes is narrower and seal time is short. Common for bars, biscuits and confectionery.
- **Box-motion / long-dwell jaws:** the jaws travel with the pack, so seal time does not depend on speed. Choose this for fresh produce, MAP packs, thicker films, or where seal integrity is critical. It runs slower than rotary.
- **Inverted wrapper (film from below):** suits loose, soft or irregular products such as fresh produce, meat on trays and bakery items.
- **Infeed options:** lugged chain, elevated lug, belt or servo infeed for unstable or delicate items.
- **Former:** fixed (one width) or adjustable (frequent changeovers).

## Key specifications to ask for
- Product L × W × H range and pack length range
- Speed in packs/min. One trade source gives about 50–150 ppm as common and 300+ ppm at the high end (typical). One long-dwell example lists 150 ppm maximum.
- Reel width and diameter, film types, seal widths
- Jaw motion type, the number of jaws ("number up"), jaw temperature control
- Gas flush, residual O2 target, print registration, coder mounting

## Sizing / selection notes
- Film width ≈ 2 × (product width + product height) + fin-seal allowance (confirm with the OEM former design).
- Pack rate × pack length gives the film speed. Check it against the film speed limit of the wrapper.
- Long-dwell machines trade speed for seal time. Ask for the seal time at your target rate.

## Integration with the line
Upstream, a feeding or orienting system (often robotic pick-and-place, or a buffer belt) must supply product at pitch with no gaps or "no product, no bag" logic. Downstream equipment usually includes a checkweigher, a metal or X-ray detector and a case packer. A leak detector or seal checker is used on MAP packs.

## Hygiene and safety notes
Interlock the end-seal jaws and knife guards. Use open infeed designs and a belt infeed for unpackaged food. Clean crumbs from the fin wheels and jaws.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Product caught in end seal | Pitch or phase wrong, product slipping on infeed | Re-phase the infeed to the jaws, check the product sensor |
| Leaky end seals | Low dwell or temperature, product debris, worn jaws | Seal settings, clean and inspect the jaws, film seal window |
| Wrinkled fin seal | Film tension, former size, fin-wheel pressure | Dancer, former setting, wheel temperature |
| Cut off-register | Dirty eye-mark sensor, film stretch | Clean and re-teach the sensor, check the film reel brake |
