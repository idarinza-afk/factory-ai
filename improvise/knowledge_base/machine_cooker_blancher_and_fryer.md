# Cookers, Blanchers and Fryers (continuous thermal processing)

## What it does / working principle
These machines carry product on a belt or through a flume while it is exposed to a heating medium for a controlled time.
- **Blancher:** a short exposure to hot water or steam to inactivate enzymes. Catalase and peroxidase are the usual indicator enzymes. Blanching also fixes colour, removes air and lowers microbial load. It is followed at once by water or air cooling. Source figures: hot water about 70–100 °C, steam about 100 °C (typical). Exact times depend on the product.
- **Continuous oven:** linear or spiral ovens use convection, impingement and/or steam to cook, roast, or colour meat, poultry, bakery and ready-meal products.
- **Continuous fryer:** product is submerged in hot oil by paddles or a submerger belt. The oil is heated directly (burner tubes, thermal fluid) or indirectly through an external heat exchanger.

## Main types and when to choose each
- **Water blancher** (rotary drum, flume, belt): uniform heating, but water-soluble nutrients leach out.
- **Steam blancher:** less leaching. Suits small or cut pieces, but some moisture is lost.
- **Linear oven:** smaller capacities, easy access. **Spiral oven:** large capacity in a small footprint.
- **Fryer with indirect heating:** tightest oil temperature control. One extension source quotes about ±1 °C for indirect heating, against several °C for direct-fired units (typical).

## Key specifications to ask for
- Capacity (kg/h), belt width and length, residence time range
- Heating medium and capacity, temperature control accuracy, airflow and humidity control (ovens)
- Oil volume and filtration (fryers), fines removal, exhaust and scrubber
- CIP or cleaning provisions, belt type

## Sizing / selection notes
- Belt area ≈ production rate × residence time ÷ belt loading (kg/m²).
- **Fryer oil turnover (h)** = oil in system ÷ oil absorbed by product per hour. Industrial fryers typically aim for about 5–10 h. A lower turnover keeps oil fresher. Use real utilisation (about 80–90%) for the actual figure.
- Cook or chill validation (core temperature targets) comes from the HACCP plan and local regulations.

## Integration with the line
Upstream: preparation, coating or batter, accumulation. Downstream: chilling or freezing (spiral), then packing. Separate raw and cooked zones (high-care/low-care) at the cooker or chiller boundary.

## Hygiene and safety notes
Fryers carry fire risk: they need fire suppression, high-limit cut-outs and oil level interlocks. Build in T-304 or better stainless, and avoid copper and brass, which speed up oil oxidation. Hot surfaces and steam need guarding. Fit exhaust hoods and scrubbers.

## Common problems and first checks
| Symptom | Likely causes | First checks |
|---|---|---|
| Uneven cook / colour | Airflow imbalance, belt loading uneven | Fans, spreader, loading density |
| Fast oil degradation | High turnover time, fines build-up, air exposure | Filtration, fines removal, oil top-up rate |
| Under-blanched product | Short time, low temperature, overloaded | Peroxidase test, belt speed, temperature |
| Low core temperature | Thicker product, belt speed too high | Product size, residence time, probe readings |
